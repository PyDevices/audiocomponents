**Module:** `audiocomponents/lib/audioinstruments/tr707.py` (146 lines)
**Proposed grade:** **literature** — a strong gold candidate was identified,

## 3. Hardware structure vs the module

**The real machine: 15 ROM sounds across 10 physical output
channels/circuits, 10-note polyphony.** Corroborated four independent
ways this session:
- Polynominal (https://www.polynominal.com/roland-tr707/): "10 channels
  polyphony," 4× HN61256P mask ROMs holding 15 samples, and an explicit
  channel table — **BD=ch1, SD=ch2, LT=ch3, MT=ch4, HT=ch5, Rimshot/Cowbell=ch6
  (shared), Clap/Tambourine=ch7 (shared), Hi-Hat=ch8 (open+closed shared),
  Crash=ch9, Ride=ch10.**
- Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TR-707): "two bass
  drums, two snares, three toms, rimshot, cowbell, clap, crash cymbal,
  ride cymbal and hi-hat (open and closed), tambourine, handclap"; "10
  notes of polyphony"; "the sounds cannot be tuned."
- BØLT's product copy (bolttracks.itch.io/707, bolttracks.com/sfx/707plus/):
  same 15-sound breakdown, plus that BD1/BD2 and SD1/SD2 are *alternates*,
  not layerable — "the alternate bass drum, snare, and hi-hat sounds
  cannot be triggered simultaneously" — i.e. they share a circuit, exactly
  matching Polynominal's one-channel-per-pair reading.
- RetroSound's listing independently counts the same total: "2x Bass
  Drum, 2x Snare Drum, 3x Toms, 2x Hats (1 Open, 1 Closed), 2x Crash
  Cymbal, 1x Clap, 1x Cowbell, 1x Rimshot, 1x Tambourine" = 15.

**The module today: 8 of the 15 sounds, 8 `NOTE_MAP` entries, and no
voice management at all.** `NOTE_MAP` (`tr707.py:32-41`) covers Kick,
Snare, Low/Mid/Hi Tom, Closed/Open Hat, Crash — 8 of 15 named sounds, and
8 of the hardware's 10 channels (missing Rimshot, Cowbell, Clap,
Tambourine, Ride entirely; BD1/BD2 and SD1/SD2 are each collapsed to one
sound). More significant: **the file declares no `MAX_VOICES` and never
calls `_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest`** (confirmed by a full-file
grep — no match). Every `NOTE_ON` unconditionally builds fresh
`synthio.Note`s and calls `synth.press()` (`tr707.py:126-130`), relying
entirely on the underlying synthio core's undocumented 14-note cap (the
same cap `tr808-evidence.md` measured and filed as
[audioif#8](https://github.com/PyDevices/audioif/issues/8)) for eviction.
And because `key_of()` (`_support.py:194-196`) keys voices by
`(channel, note_id or pitch)`, the only choking that happens today is a
*note retriggering itself* — Closed Hat (42) and Open Hat (46) do **not**
choke each other under the current code, even though they physically
share one circuit on the real machine (`tr707.py:107-111`: both land in
one `elif`, but each gets its own key).

**Proposed structural change**, justified entirely by §3's hardware
facts: rebuild as **10 fixed circuits**, one per hardware channel,
following the pattern `tr808-evidence.md` proved out (permanent
`synthio.Note`s retriggered in place, never allocated at strike time,
choking modeled as the hardware's own circuit sharing rather than as an
afterthought). `TOTAL RESIDENT NOTES ≤ 13` per the charter (synthio's
14-note core cap, one below it as `tr808-evidence.md` did). Proposed
budget:

| # | Channel | MIDI notes | Sounds | Notes (synthio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BD | 36, 35 | Bass Drum 1, Bass Drum 2 | 2 (tone + click) |
| 2 | SD | 38, 40 | Snare 1, Snare 2 | 2 (body + noise snap) |
| 3 | LT | 45 | Low Tom | 1 |
| 4 | MT | 47 | Mid Tom | 1 |
| 5 | HT | 50 | Hi Tom | 1 |
| 6 | RS/CB | 37, 56 | Rimshot, Cowbell | 1 (shared, retuned per hit) |
| 7 | CP/TA | 39, 54 | Hand Clap, Tambourine | 1 (shared, retuned per hit) |
| 8 | HH | 42, 46 | Closed Hat, Open Hat | 1 (shared, chokes) |
| 9 | CY | 49 | Crash | 2 (tone + noise) |
| 10 | RD | 51 | Ride | 1 |
| | | | **Total** | **13** |

15 named sounds, 10 real circuits, 13 `synthio.Note`s — matches the
hardware's own polyphony count exactly (10), and lands one below the
core cap the same way tr808's rebuild did. `35` (GM "Acoustic Bass
Drum") and `40` (GM "Electric Snare") are unused anywhere else in
`audioinstruments` (checked by grepping every `NOTE_MAP` in the
directory); `37`/`39`/`54`/`56`/`51` already match the sibling convention
used by `linndrum.py`, `tr909.py`, `dmx.py`, `cr78.py` (Rimshot=37,
Clap=39, Tambourine=54, Cowbell=56, Ride=51 — all confirmed by grep across
the directory). Low/Mid/Hi Tom stay at the module's existing 45/47/50
(its own established convention, shared with `drumtraks.py`/`sp1200.py`;
changing them was not asked for and is not justified by anything found
this run).

## 4. Modeling approach: synthesis-to-match, not sample playback

The charter is explicit and the hardware agrees: this is a ROMpler
(4× mask ROM, no analog signal path to model as a circuit), so there is
no DAFx-style circuit paper to re-derive and none should be expected —
the existing file's own comment already gestures at the right target
("TR-707 used early PCM, so drums sound a bit metallic/FM-like compared
to analog," `tr707.py:53`). Each of the 10 circuits above is synthesized
from primitives and tuned against measurement, the same discipline
`tr808.md` used for its analog circuits, applied here to a digital one.

**The 6-bit/low-rate character, stated as a target.** Sources disagree on
scope: Polynominal states the ROMs hold "15 samples at 6 bits resolution"
(all of them); BØLT's page is more specific — "digital memory... stored
in resolutions of 8-bit for drums and percussion, and 6-bit for
cymbals." Both agree the cymbal-family voices (Crash, Ride, and by
extension the noise-heavy Hi-Hat) are the lowest-resolution samples on
the machine, which is exactly why the module already reaches for
`FM_BELL` there. Proposed measurable target: the cymbal-family voices'
release tail should plateau at a noise floor no cleaner than roughly
**-30 to -40 dBFS relative to peak** (6-bit ≈ 36 dB of quantization
headroom, per the standard 6.02·n dB rule) rather than decaying
arbitrarily quiet — measurable as the RMS floor of the last ~20 ms of a
rendered tail. **Not measured this session** — no clean capture was
obtained to confirm the real floor; this is a literature-derived target
for Station C to check once a render exists, not a verified number.

**The decay curve should stay smooth, not stepped.** BØLT's page states
the actual mechanism: "a trick that Roland employed to avoid low bitrate
dithering artifacts during sample decay was using analog envelope
generators that attenuate the individual sounds after the 707's D/A
converters" — i.e. the ROM data is short and the graceful decay is an
*analog* VCA stage downstream of it, not more ROM. (This claim is sourced
to BØLT's product copy, not independently confirmed against the service
notes PDF this session — no PDF text-extraction tool was available in
this environment, and the PDF was only format-validated, not mined.)
Practically this validates the module's existing approach: continuous
`synthio.Envelope`/`LFO` decay shapes, not an attempt to simulate a
truncated digital tail. Keep it.

**Per-circuit approach:**
- **BD** (36/35): tone (`SINE`, current approach at `tr707.py:92-95`) +
  a short HP-filtered noise click for PCM attack character, the way
  `tr808.py`'s bass drum pairs a tuned body with a noise-click Note.
  BD1/BD2 share the circuit and differ only by retune (pitch/click-mix)
  at trigger time — no capture distinguishes them this session, so
  Station B should pick an audibly-distinct-but-related pair rather than
  invent one from nothing.
- **SD** (38/40): keep the existing body+noise-snap structure
  (`tr707.py:97-105`), retuned per SD1/SD2 the same way.
- **LT/MT/HT** (45/47/50): keep the existing tone+bend structure
  (`tr707.py:113-117`); recalibrate the three target Hz against §5.
- **RS/CB** (37/56): one shared Note, retuned at trigger time — rimshot
  a short noisy/woody click, cowbell a metallic tone reusing `FM_BELL`
  near the tr808 sibling's cowbell target (~500-635 Hz, corroborated by
  the Studio Brootle Cow Bell measurement below).
- **CP/TA** (39/54): one shared Note, retuned at trigger time — this is
  a real cut from `tr909.py`'s 3-Note clap (`tr909.py:207-212`); the
  13-note budget does not afford it here, the same trade
  `tr808-evidence.md` made for its own clap/cowbell.
- **HH** (42/46): keep the existing noise+HP structure
  (`tr707.py:107-111`) but move it onto the shared-circuit key so open
  and closed **actually choke each other** — the one outright behavioral
  bug this survey found in the current file.
- **CY** (49): keep the existing tone+noise pair
  (`tr707.py:119-124`); consider `audiodynamics.Dynamics` transient
  shaping on the attack instead of relying solely on envelope `attack_time`,
  per the toolkit upgrade `tr808.md` already recommended for this family.
- **RD** (51, new): one Note, tighter/more tonal than Crash (Ride
  typically reads as more pitched, less noisy) — the 1-Note budget here
  versus Crash's 2 is the explicit trade that makes the 13-note total
  work; `tr909.py:197-205` shows the sibling's fuller 2-Note treatment
  of both cymbals for contrast, not as something this budget can afford.

No convolution is proposed anywhere in this instrument; no
`audioconvolve.FRAMES` latency is incurred.

## 6. MCU budget and macro budget

**MCU:** 13 permanently-resident `synthio.Note` objects across the 10
fixed circuits (§3), each retriggered rather than allocated per hit —
structurally *simpler* for the MCU than today's unbounded
allocate-and-hope code, and the same shape (2-3 Notes per hit, a handful
of `Biquad`s) `tr808.md` budgeted and passed. **No `" - lean"` patch is
expected.**

**Macros: 8 today, 13 proposed, 3 free of 16.** The panel evidence
justifies the expansion precisely: TR-707's front panel carries one
level knob per output channel (confirmed via search of Sweetwater's and
RetroSound's technical-specifications coverage: "individual output level
controls allow you to adjust the volume of each drum voice
independently," matching the rear panel's per-channel multi-outputs) —
"the only sound parameter available on the 707 is volume" (same source).
So every *Level* macro below traces to a real panel control, one per
hardware channel; nothing traces to a per-voice tune or decay pot,
because the real machine has none.

| # | Label | Mode | Traces to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Volume | UNIPOLAR | master output (kept) |
| 1 | Bass Drum Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 1 knob (renamed from "Kick Level") |
| 2 | Snare Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 2 knob (kept) |
| 3 | Low Tom Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 3 knob (split out of "Tom Level") |
| 4 | Mid Tom Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 4 knob (split out of "Tom Level") |
| 5 | Hi Tom Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 5 knob (split out of "Tom Level") |
| 6 | Rimshot/Cowbell Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 6 knob (new) |
| 7 | Clap/Tambourine Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 7 knob (new) |
| 8 | HiHat Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 8 knob (kept) |
| 9 | Crash Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 9 knob (kept) |
| 10 | Ride Level | UNIPOLAR | channel 10 knob (new) |
| 11 | Overall Decay | UNIPOLAR | **not** a panel control — synthesis-calibration global, kept from today's macro since the machine has no per-voice decay pot to fold it onto |
| 12 | Master Tune | BIPOLAR | **not** a panel control — "the sounds cannot be tuned" per Wikipedia; kept as a synthesis-calibration global for the same reason `tr808.md` needed one |

Net: 5 new macros against the charter's 8 free slots, 3 left over. If a
future acceptance pass wants a 14th, there is room without a contract
escalation.
