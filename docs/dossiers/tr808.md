## 3. Hardware structure vs the module

12 analog drum voices per Wikipedia's infobox (polyphony 12 / timbrality
12): BD, SD, low/mid/hi tom, rimshot, hand clap, cowbell, cymbal,
open/closed hi-hat, congas, claves, maracas — several sharing physical
circuits (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TR-808). `NOTE_MAP`
(tr808.py:42-56) has 12 entries but **omits congas and maracas**: their
MIDI notes fall to the generic noise+bandpass "Fallback" branch
(tr808.py:244-248), not a dedicated voice. Sibling modules set a
note-number convention worth reusing: `linndrum.py:55-57` maps Conga
Hi/Mid/Lo to 62/63/64; `cr78.py:55` maps Maracas to 70.

Proposed structural change: give Congas (62/63/64) and Maracas (70)
dedicated branches, driven by the measured targets below (Congas n=30,
Maracas n=11), in place of the fallback — additive within the existing
12-voice count, not an expansion of it.

`MAX_VOICES = 16` (tr808.py:115) is a **synthio.Note pool**, not a
hardware-voice count, so headroom over 12 looks fine on its face — but
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest` (`_support.py:239-257`) compare
`len(voices)` (held hit-*groups*) against `max_voices`, mixing units with
the incoming hit's raw Note count. Several voices are multi-oscillator
internally (SD=3, Clap=3, Cymbal=2, BD=2, Rimshot=2, Cowbell=2;
tr808.py:161-227), so a full-kit hit wants ≈20 Notes against a pool of
16 — pre-existing, not introduced here. Station B should verify stealing
behavior under a dense pattern rather than assume 16 raw oscillators of
headroom.

## 4. Modeling approach, by voice group

**Bass drum** (tr808.py:150-160): the DAFx-14 mechanism, re-derived from
its text, not copied — trigger → pulse shaper → bridged-T bandpass
network with a feedback buffer; a retrigger pulse re-injected a few ms
after attack raises the center frequency **more than an octave** briefly
(the attack "click/jump"), then feedback leakage produces a slow downward
pitch "sigh." VR6 varies feedback amount and, with it, decay time.
Roland's tuning chart nominal is 56 Hz, consistent with the measured
53.8 Hz median below.

The current model only bends *down* from `bd_tune` via a one-shot `FALL`
LFO — it never jumps up first. Proposed: a two-stage bend (fast upward
jump >1 octave over ~2-5 ms, then exponential settle toward `bd_tune`),
with `bd_decay` driving settle tau — folding "VR6 feedback → decay" onto
the existing macro, no new one needed. The existing noise-click Note
(tr808.py:157-159) already mirrors the paper's separate pulse-shaper
stage; keep it, tighten HP cutoff/decay against the numbers below.

**Snare / toms / hats / cymbal / clap / cowbell / rimshot**
(tr808.py:161-227): existing multi-oscillator structures (tuned body +
noise-through-bandpass) already match the published block-diagram shape
for TR-808 percussion; no DAFx paper was found for these voices
specifically. MIT-licensed io-808 corroborates the general architecture
(https://github.com/vincentriemer/io-808) — read for shape, not ported.
Toolkit upgrade: swap the fixed-Q `synthio.Biquad` bandpass stages for
the corrected wide biquad with honest band edges (hat/cymbal bands sit
close to where the old biquad's error matters most); consider
`audiodynamics` transient shaping on the clap's triple-attack stack in
place of manually staggered `attack_time` offsets. No convolution is
proposed — no `audioconvolve.FRAMES` latency incurred.

**Measurement method:** `tools/measure_hits.py` on rendered one-shot WAVs
per voice — the same script that produced these targets, so Station C's
numbers are computed by the same code that proposed them.

The loudness rows were added on 2026-09-02, after §7 found a 15 dB level
error in the hats that every row above it passed cleanly. They are read
with the program's shared ruler (ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting over a fixed
400 ms window from onset) on the same velocity-100 one-shots, and the
targets are this pack's own per-voice medians, resampled to 48 kHz in
memory and measured in place. A machine's own capture outranks the
cross-kit house norm where the two disagree, and here they do: the house
CY−BD median is +4.9 dB against this pack's −0.6 dB.

A loudness row cannot, on its own, tell a faithful rebuild from a gain
trim — makeup gain is spectrum-neutral and would satisfy it while holding
every tau and centroid row above. Read it alongside the spectral flatness
figures in §7, which a gain change does not move.

## 6. MCU budget and macro budget

Oscillator counts stay as-is per voice group (2-3 Notes per hit, as
today); the BD change trades one `LFO` shape for a two-stage bend, adding
no new oscillators. No `" - lean"` patch is expected — the added
Congas/Maracas branches reuse noise/tone building blocks already in the
file, not new DSP classes.

`MACRO_LABELS` is already at the 16/16 ceiling (tr808.py:9-13). **No new
macro is proposed.** The BD attack-jump/sigh folds onto the existing `BD
Decay` macro; Congas/Maracas get fixed relative tuning within the
existing model rather than a dedicated tune macro, consistent with the
hardware's shared-circuit voices (survey: "several sharing physical
circuits"). If a future acceptance pass finds this insufficient,
escalate a 17th-macro ask to Brad rather than silently exceed the
ceiling (roadmap §3, R6).

Table RAM, revised 2026-09-02: the METAL bank (§7) still occupies one
2048-sample `array("h")`, 4 KB, unchanged — the correction changed the
table's *contents*, not the number of tables. It now builds inside
`create()` rather than at import, so the cost is paid on the first
instrument at a given sample rate and `make_table`'s argument cache
serves every one after it. Measured cold build on the workspace
MicroPython (unix port, `ulab` present): 6.5 ms, against 0.2 ms cached.
The hats also gave up their per-sample `Biquad`, which the closed hat —
the most frequently struck voice in the kit — pays on every block.

## 7. Metal bank correction — hi-hats and cymbal (2026-09-02)

**What was wrong.** The closed hat measured −42.7 LUFS and the open hat
−34.2 on the program's shared ruler, against −23.8..−32.1 and
−18.7..−26.7 for the other nine kits' hats, and −26.8 / −15.9 for this
machine's own MusicRadar reference. Every criterion in §5 passed while
that was true, because §5 had no loudness row. Three causes, all in the
METAL bank, all measured:

1. **A truncated harmonic series.** `_METAL_PARTS` drove each square
   oscillator with `h in (1, 3, 5)`. A square wave's series does not stop
   at the fifth harmonic, and the truncation left 16 distinct partials in
   the whole table with 96.9% of the bank's power below the hi-hat's 8 kHz
   high-pass. Spectral flatness over 4–16 kHz — the band the hat lives in
   — measured 0.008 for the closed hat and 0.000 for the open hat and
   cymbal, against 0.302 / 0.116 / 0.114 for the reference pack. The model
   was making a metallic chord where the machine makes a dense hiss.

2. **The wrong octave.** `METAL_HZ` was 90.0, putting the bank at
   900–2700 Hz. The module's own comment cites the hardware bank as
   ~205/304/369/421/497/619 Hz, which against these multipliers is a
   least-squares fit of Σ(f·m)/Σ(m²) = 20.495 Hz — the code contradicted
   its own comment by two octaves. That is also what made the spectrum
   sparse: given the full square series below, 90 Hz puts 7 of the six
   oscillators' partials in the 6–11 kHz band; 20.5 Hz puts 35.

3. **Peak normalisation before the filter.** `make_table` normalises to
   peak, and the wavetable is the only gain stage these voices have —
   synthio clamps `Note.amplitude` at 1.0
   (`audioif/src/cpython/synthio.py:396-398`) and the kit hands the
   synthesizer straight to `Instrument` with no mixer or filter stage
   downstream (tr808.py:436). A table normalised
   *before* the band is applied spends its headroom on the oscillator
   fundamentals the hi-hat immediately discards; the 8 kHz high-pass then
   threw away 14.3 dB that nothing downstream could return. Raising
   `amp * 0.7` was never a fix: at 0.83 a fully accented hit already
   reaches the clamp, so the whole available makeup was 1.5 dB against a
   15 dB deficit.

**What changed**, all in `lib/audioinstruments/tr808.py`. No public
signature, macro number, `MACRO_LABELS` entry, `NOTE_MAP` assignment or
patch value moved, and the kit still resides at exactly 13 permanent
`synthio.Note` objects.

- METAL is built in `create()` by `_metal_table(sample_rate)` rather than
  at import, and its series is capped below both the table's harmonic
  ceiling and Nyquist. It is now alias-free at 22.05 kHz as well as 48;
  the shipped table's top partial aliased at any rate below 27 kHz.
- Each oscillator gets its full odd-harmonic square series — what the
  module's comment already claimed. 16 distinct partials become 114 at
  48 kHz.
- The hi-hat channel's band is applied *while the table is built*:
  Butterworth magnitudes, 4th-order high-pass at 5 kHz and 2nd-order
  low-pass at 13 kHz. Peak normalisation then lands on the signal the
  voices actually play, which is what the hardware's post-filter output
  stage does and the only place a fixed-point wavetable can carry it.
- Harmonics of different oscillators that land on the same table index
  are summed in **power**, not amplitude: six free-running oscillators do
  not phase-lock, so their coincidences do not add coherently. The
  coherent sum was manufacturing single lines several dB too strong — it
  is why `f_early` read exactly 8098 Hz for the closed hat, the open hat
  *and* the cymbal, three voices reporting one artifact.
- Partial signs are scattered from a seeded LCG (`noise_table`'s own
  generator, seed 687 — lowest crest of seeds 1..2500 at 48 kHz).
  `make_table` sums every partial in sine phase, which piles the bank's
  peaks onto one table index for 20.4 dB of crest; the reference pack's
  own hat hits measure 9–12 dB over their first 20 ms, so the aligned
  table was the outlier and the scatter is the correction. Phase is a
  free parameter here — the magnitude spectrum is untouched — and the
  scatter returns ~12 dB of headroom.
- Hats: no runtime filter, since the band is now in the table and this is
  the voice whose band sits highest; `amp * 0.7` → `amp * 0.8`.
- Cymbal: filter unchanged (`BAND_PASS` 8000 Hz Q=1.2); `amp * 0.85` →
  `amp * 0.8`. 0.8 is the largest multiplier that keeps a fully accented
  hit (amp 1.21 at velocity 127) under the amplitude clamp.

**Known residual — the cymbal is brighter than the machine.** The bank is
voiced once, at the hi-hat's band, because the hat is the voice whose band
sits highest and can least afford a second pass; the cymbal takes its
share at runtime, which it can afford. What it cannot do is recover the
low-mid content the voicing removed: the reference cymbal carries 15.3% of
its power at 2–3 kHz and 11.6% at 3–4 kHz, ours carries 0.1% and 1.3%, and
its centroid reads 9112 against the pack's 5238 (and 6177 before this
change). Level, tau and f_early all land, and §5 states no cymbal centroid
criterion, so this is recorded rather than silently accepted. The fix is a
second voiced table for the cymbal's own band at +4 KB of table RAM —
worth taking if the cymbal reads thin on a listen against the pack.

**Also unresolved, found while measuring and outside this correction:**
Hi Tom `f_early` reads 187.5 Hz against the pooled 129 Hz ±30% row
(90.3–167.7) and Conga Hi reads 363.3 Hz against 258 Hz ±30%
(180.6–335.4). Both fail today and both failed before this change — the
pooled-median rows in §5 cannot hold three pitches of a tuned voice at
once. Either the rows want per-pitch targets or the tunings want a look.
