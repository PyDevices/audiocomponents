## 3. Hardware structure vs the module

**The polyphony fact, sourced exactly:** Wikipedia states "The CR-78
offers 14 sounds, with four-note polyphony (meaning it can produce up to
four sounds at the same time)," attached to a footnote (re-confirmed this
session). The 14-sound list separately names **High conga, Low conga,
High bongo, Low bongo** as four distinct tuned-percussion voices (plus
Kick, Snare, Rimshot, Hi-hat, Cymbal and — per the module's own remaining
`NOTE_MAP` entries — Tambourine, Metal Beat, Cowbell, Guiro, Maracas,
Claves).

**Independently corroborated by the service notes, not just asserted from
Wikipedia:** the manual's own factory "RHYTHM VOICE" adjustment procedure
(OCR'd, `.reference-captures/cr78/service_notes_djvu.txt` lines ~6684-7170)
lists **BD, SD, RS, HH, CY, HB (High Bongo), LB (Low Bongo), LC, and CB
(Cowbell)** as separate rows, each with its own dedicated trim
potentiometer(s) (e.g. "VR51... VR52" for HB, "VR53... VR54" for LB,
"VR55... VR56" for **LC** — a *third*, distinct tuned-percussion row,
consistent with Wikipedia's separate Low Conga). A later line groups three
more voices for one adjustment step: *"Slide ADD VOICE knobs upward, {Tb,
GU, MB, respectively}"* (Tambourine, Guiro, Metal Beat). This is real,
independent, primary-source corroboration of two things: (a) each named
voice has its **own dedicated analog circuit** (not a shared-generator
architecture like `tr909`'s hi-hat/cymbal — no CR-78-specific evidence of
circuit sharing was found anywhere in the OCR text), and (b) **the
hardware likely has more tuned-percussion voices than the module's
`NOTE_MAP` currently carries.**

**A real, evidenced gap:** the module's `NOTE_MAP` (cr78.py:42-57) has
only two conga/bongo-family entries — `(60, "Bongo Hi")`, `(61, "Bongo
Lo")` (cr78.py:53-54) — where Wikipedia names four and the service notes'
own HB/LB/LC rows independently support at least three. This dossier does
**not** propose adding the missing voice(s): doing so needs a new
`NOTE_MAP` entry and, to stay useful, a new macro, and `MACRO_LABELS` is
already at 16/16 (§7) — noted here for whoever next revisits this
instrument, not solved in this minimal-change plan.

**What the manual does *not* explain, searched for explicitly and not
found:** the mechanism behind the 4-simultaneous ceiling. The OCR text was
searched for "simultaneous," "same time," "voice," "block diagram,"
"priority," "matrix," "latch," and "gate" (see the raw hits above). The
closest matches — **"ADD VOICE"** (a physical slider, part number
`LYE6B001-10KB`, labelled "VOL. ADD VOICE" in the switches list) and
**"CANCEL VOICE"** (a push-switch, `SUF53`, "CLEAR, CANCEL VOICE") — read
in context (lines 2260-2270, 7165-7166, 7649) as **rhythm-programming**
controls: selecting which instruments participate in a pattern via the
rotary `INSTRUMENT SELECTOR`, and clearing/cancelling one instrument's
programmed pattern during composition — not a real-time voice-steal
description. **Not found, stated honestly rather than guessed at:**
whether a 5th simultaneous trigger on real hardware is dropped, steals the
oldest voice, or does something else. §6 proposes a mechanism; it is not
sourced to this manual.

**Module today:** `MAX_VOICES = 16` (cr78.py:100) is a `synthio.Note`-pool
ceiling with no relationship to the hardware's real ceiling — 4x too high,
the mismatch the task flagged. Per Brad's "if it was 4-voice in real
life, it's 4-voice here" rule, §6 proposes a real-time 4-voice arbiter
replacing it.

## 4. The two budgets, and why CR-78 needs both

`tr909`/`tr707`/`sp1200` each needed only **one** number: how many
permanent circuits fit under the audioif-wide 13-note residency ceiling
(synthio's 14-note program-wide cap, minus one for `audioif#8`/`#9`'s
at-cap re-press bugs). Their hardware's own simultaneity ceiling and their
resident-circuit count were the same number (or close enough that no
separate arbiter was needed) — hit any pattern you like, everything can
sound at once, because that is what those machines' hardware did.

**CR-78 is the first Phase 1 drum where these two numbers genuinely
differ.** 14 named sounds (≈13-14 dedicated analog circuits, §3) need to
be *resident* so any of them can fire; but the reference caps *audible
simultaneity* at 4, well below that. Two separate mechanisms are needed:

1. **Residency (§5):** how many permanent `synthio.Note` objects exist,
   ≤13 (the audioif-wide ceiling, unchanged from the other three drums).
2. **The real-time arbiter (§6):** how many of those may be in an active
   (non-silent) envelope phase at once, capped at the reference's own 4 —
   a genuinely new mechanism this program hasn't needed before.

## 5. The FIXED-CIRCUIT map (mandatory)

Following the proven `tr909`/`sp1200` idiom exactly (`circuits = {}` cache
keyed by voice name, `circuit(name, waveforms)` builds each permanent
`synthio.Note` tuple once and returns the cached tuple on every later hit
— see `tr909.py:127-135` for the pattern this reuses verbatim): every
voice is a **permanent** `synthio.Note` group, parameters reset and
`synth.press()`-retriggered in place on every hit, matching how the real
machine's voltage-triggered analog circuits work — nothing allocated at
strike time, nothing freed mid-play.

**Amended after the re-bless listen — the SD fallback below was TAKEN.**

*Provenance of the trigger, stated exactly.* At the re-bless listen of
2026-09-02 Brad declined to bless the **cr78 kit** ("all are blessed except
cr78"). He did not name a voice, and he had approved this same snare at the
Phase 1 batch listen on 2026-09-01 (commit `2e503da`). So there is **no
by-ear judgement of the snare specifically** on record, and this dossier does
not claim one. The trigger that identified the snare is the **cross-kit
loudness grid**, not an ear.

What the grid says: the shipped voice rendered at **-46.7 LUFS** against a
**-21.1 LUFS** kick in its own kit — the largest outlier in the program, and
20-25 dB below every other kit's snare (-19 to -27). The cause is structural,
not a trim: band-passing broadband noise at 240 Hz with Q 3.1 discards
**26.3 dB** of the source's power, and `synthio` clamps `Note.amplitude` at
1.0, so *this* topology cannot make it back.

Two alternatives were built and refuted by measurement before the two-Note
design was accepted, and neither refutation is a taste call:

- **One Note, wider filter.** A single noise Note through HIGH_PASS 1800 Hz
  reaches -22.5 LUFS — on target. It carries **1.1%** of its energy below
  500 Hz against the field's 23-45%: no body at all.
- **One Note, filter cascade** (HIGH_PASS ~200 Hz for body + PEAKING_EQ at
  `SD Pitch` for snap, which costs no residency since audioif takes four
  biquads per Note). Swept across corner, gain and level: every design lands
  at **1.5-2.0%** below 500 Hz, and the peaking stage barely moves it. Boosting
  a narrow band of flat noise boosts ~2% of the signal. **Body comes from a
  tone oscillator, not from filtered noise** — which is why every healthy
  snare in this library is tone+noise.

So the earlier claim that "the one-Note topology's hard ceiling is -41.8 LUFS"
is **withdrawn as stated**: it is true of the shipped band-pass, not of one-Note
snares generally. The argument for the second oscillator is **timbre, not
level**.
**`SD Snappy` after the change, measured across its full range** (0-127, at
velocity 100) — recorded here because the macro now moves level as well as
timbre and that should not be discovered by surprise:

| Snappy | LUFS | energy above 2 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | -26.7 | 0.0% |
| 32 | -24.8 | 41.2% |
| 64 | -21.7 | 71.7% |
| 96 | -19.0 | 83.0% |
| 127 | -16.9 | 87.7% |

It sweeps timbre across essentially the whole range, which is what the macro is
for and what the old design could not do at all. It also carries a **9.8 dB
level swing**, wider than the 6.4 dB the old design had. At Snappy 0 the voice
degenerates to a bare unfiltered 240 Hz sine — body with no rattle whatever —
which is a legitimate setting to be able to reach but is not a snare. The
default of 0.4 sits at -23.0 LUFS with 59.8% above 2 kHz.

Repurposing `SD Snappy` as the filter's Q also ran the macro **backwards**:
with no high-frequency path in the circuit at all, turning Snappy up could only
make the voice louder (6.5 dB across its range), never snappier. The table
below is the map **as built**, with the fallback applied exactly as it was
written — SD back to 2 Notes, funded by the Claves+Cowbell merge, **not** by
unmerging the Hat.

**Why no merge was used, and why cr78 now holds 14 Notes.** The Claves+Cowbell
merge the SD row nominates was implemented in full and then withdrawn. `synthio`
takes the *reattack* path for a note that is still sounding and does **not**
rebuild its envelope state from the newly assigned `note.envelope`, so two
voices sharing one circuit swap decays whenever they overlap:

| | alone | overlapped, merged |
|---|---|---|
| Claves | 53 ms | **207 ms** — inherits the Cowbell's tail |
| Cowbell | 203 ms | **55 ms** — cut to the Claves' tail |

Both are correct again at 500 ms separation, which confirms the mechanism, and
both are correct at HEAD where the circuits are separate. CR-78 Latin patterns
play claves and cowbell together, so this is audible, and it is a *new* defect
rather than a redistribution of an old one.

cr78 therefore carries **14 permanent Notes across 13 circuits**, one over the
house residency guideline, and that is the deliberate choice. It is safe here
because the 4-voice arbiter never presses more than five Notes at once:
firing all 14 pitches in a single block, three rounds at velocity 127, holds
steady at 14 residents with no growth and peaks at 28910 of 32767.

**This same flaw is latent in the Hat circuit**, which was already shared before
this change — an open hat struck shortly after a closed one inherits the closed
hat's short decay. It is not introduced here and is not fixed here; it is
recorded so the next person does not rediscover it as a mystery.

## 6. The real-time 4-voice arbiter (the hardware's own limit, layered on top)

This dossier proposes **reusing the module's own existing
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest`/`release_voice` machinery
unchanged** (already imported at cr78.py:61-66, already wired at
cr78.py:105-116) — just feeding it the **cached, permanent** circuit Note
tuples from §5 instead of freshly-constructed ones (the functions are
agnostic to that distinction: `_support.py:199-214` and `:252-261` just
press/release whatever `notes` tuple they're handed), and changing what
`k` identifies (circuit name, not raw MIDI pitch, so Closed/Open Hat
collapse onto the Hat circuit's one arbiter slot if §5's merge holds).

**The number is 5, not 4 — verified by hand-tracing the existing loop,
not assumed:** `trigger_voice`'s stealing check is `while len(voices) +
len(notes) >= max_voices` (`_support.py:255`). With every CR-78 trigger
pressing exactly 1 note (true uniformly once SD and Guiro are both
reduced to 1 Note in §5 — a second reason for those two reductions,
beyond the residency budget), tracing five consecutive distinct triggers
against `MAX_VOICES = 4` lands at a **steady-state of 3** simultaneously
held voices (the check fires and steals down to 2 *before* the 4th is
even admitted). `MAX_VOICES = 5` is what actually realizes Wikipedia's
cited 4-voice ceiling: the 5th distinct trigger's check fires while only
3 are held, steals down to 2, then presses, landing at 4 held — matching
the reference. Retriggering an *already-held* circuit doesn't consume a
new slot (`trigger_voice` calls `release(k)` unconditionally first,
popping the same key before the count check), so hitting the same drum
twice never steals a different voice — the same behavior a
voltage-triggered analog circuit would show.

**Amended with the SD fallback (§5): the arbiter now counts CIRCUITS, not
Notes.** This paragraph's hand-trace was exact only under its own stated
premise — *"every CR-78 trigger pressing exactly 1 note (true uniformly once
SD and Guiro are both reduced to 1 Note in §5)"*. Restoring SD's second
oscillator breaks that premise, and `_support.trigger_voice`'s admission check
is note-counted (`while len(voices) + len(notes) >= max_voices`,
`_support.py:255`), so a 2-Note snare would steal **two** voices per hit and
cap the steady state at **3** — below the hardware's own 4. Verified by
running it, not argued: with the note-counted check and the 2-Note SD, the
trigger sequence BD → Bongo Hi → Bongo Lo → Hat → Snare lands **3** circuits
held; counting circuits instead lands **4**. So `cr78.py`'s own
`trigger_voice` wrapper now does the admission count itself — `release_voice(k)`,
then `while len(voices) >= MAX_VOICES - 1: steal_oldest(...)`, then press —
while `steal_oldest`/`release_voice` stay the shared machinery, untouched.
`_support.py` was **not** modified. **This is a deviation from this section's
"machinery unchanged" wording, recorded rather than glossed**; note that for
every 1-Note voice the two forms are algebraically identical (both reduce to
"steal while 4 are held"), so nothing but the snare changes behavior, and
`MAX_VOICES` stays 5.

**Flagged plainly:** §3 found no description of the real mechanism.
Steal-oldest is proposed because it is the module's own already-tested
idiom, not because the manual specifies it. If the batch listen finds
stealing wrong (e.g. a real CR-78 might simply drop the 5th trigger
instead), swapping `trigger_voice`'s call for a no-op-on-full-arbiter is a
small, isolated change — the same "flagged, not asserted, degrades
gracefully" posture `tr909.md` used for its crash/ride choke.

| Hat/Cymbal | decay 0.05-0.8 s escalating CH<OH<CY; high-pass 4-10 kHz (`Hat Tone`, line 250) | manual's HH row: *"Adjusting VR60 on any one VOICE — makes all"* (quoted directly) — textual evidence of a shared or closely-linked trim across the hat family |
| Bongo Hi/Lo | 300-600 Hz / 200-400 Hz (lines 242-243) | order-of-magnitude consistent with the manual's HB/LB rows (numeric confidence low — table order corrupted in OCR, §3) |
| Claves | band-pass ~2200 Hz, decay 0.05 s | no legible manual row found under this name; value carried forward unchanged |
| Cowbell | band-pass ~800 Hz, decay 0.2 s | manual's CB row exists (its own VR pair, Q529/Q530); numeric target not legibly extracted |
| Guiro | ratchet texture (§5 redesign) | today's 5-Note stagger is the module's own prior design, not schematically sourced; the redesign target is a behavioral match (bump count/spacing), not a specific Hz |
| Tambourine | high-pass ~6000 Hz, decay 0.15 s | grouped with GU/MB in the manual's own adjustment step (§3); no separate numeric row found |
| Maracas | high-pass ~5000 Hz, decay 0.05 s | same grouping as Tambourine |
| Metal Beat | high-pass ~4000 Hz, decay 0.08 s, tone ~600 Hz | same grouping as Tambourine/Guiro |

**Loudness — the criterion this dossier did not have, and the hole the SD
defect fell through.** Every acceptance row above is timbre or envelope only,
so a voice sitting 25 dB below its own kit could pass the whole table cleanly,
and did. Measured on the shared ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighted ruler over a fixed
400 ms window from onset, on the same velocity-100 solo one-shots §7 already
renders:

| Voice | Target (LUFS) | Accept | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| SD | -22.5 | -24.5 … -20.5 | **derived, not measured** — no CR-78 snare capture exists in any source reached (§1), so the target is the cross-kit SD-minus-BD median (-1.3 dB) applied to cr78's own BD (-21.1), cross-checked against the only measured machine SD/BD offset in the program (-3.4 dB, MusicRadar TR-808) |
| every other voice | within the -15 … -34 LUFS band the other nine kits occupy | ±3 dB of its own kit-median neighbours | house cross-kit norm; cr78's remaining 13 voices already sit inside it |

**Stated plainly so a future gate does not mistake one for the other:** a
loudness row alone cannot tell a faithful rebuild from a bare gain trim. Pure
makeup gain is spectrum-neutral — it would satisfy a level target while leaving
tau, centroid and `f_early` exactly where they were. For SD the discriminator
has to be structural, and here it is: the old voice put **93.4%** of its energy
below 500 Hz and **0.4%** above 4 kHz, which is a resonant thump, not a snare.

With 11 of 13 voices carrying only literature-tier, mostly-unchanged
targets, Station C's before/after delta for this instrument will mostly
be measuring the **structural** change (residency, the 4-voice arbiter)
rather than large envelope/spectral deltas — stated as the honest
expectation going in, not manufactured as a finding.

## 8. MCU budget and macro budget

13 resident Notes across 12 circuits (§5) — the same order as `tr909` (12)
and `sp1200` (12); **no `" - lean"` patch expected.** The Guiro redesign
(5→1 Notes, strong precedent) is still the single biggest saving. Of the two
lower-confidence calls that made up the rest, **one was refused and one was
not**: the SD reduction is undone (§5), the Hat merge stands. The Note that
pays for SD's restored oscillator comes from the Claves+Cowbell merge, exactly
as the SD row nominated — so the ceiling is unmoved and the total is unchanged
at 13, verified by instrumenting `synthio.Note.__init__` at runtime rather than
counted by eye. The arbiter (§6) adds no new audioif nodes — it reuses
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest`/`release_voice`, already imported
(cr78.py:61-66), against a 5-entry-max dict instead of freely growing one.

`MACRO_LABELS` stays at **16/16** (cr78.py:9-13) — already at the
ceiling; **no new macro proposed.** Direct manual support exists for BD,
SD, RS, Bongo Hi, Bongo Lo, Cowbell, and Hat Tone (§3, §7); Accent follows
standard rhythm-composer convention and was not independently confirmed
in the sources read this session; the remaining "X Level" macros
(Claves, Guiro, Tambourine, Maracas, Metal Beat) are plausible but
likewise not independently confirmed. **One nuance worth recording
plainly:** the manual's switches list shows the real panel used a single
physical "VOL./ADD VOICE" slider together with the rotary `INSTRUMENT
SELECTOR` — select a voice, then trim its level on one shared control —
rather than 14 simultaneously-live knobs. Today's 16 independently-live
macros already exceed that literal 1970s ergonomics. That's explicitly
allowed under the macro-freedom rule (roadmap §3, vision §2.2) and is a
usability improvement, not a fidelity claim — recorded here only so it
isn't mistaken for a sourced 1:1 panel mapping.

**Not solved here, recorded for the next revisit:** §3's conga/bongo gap
(the reference names 4 tuned-percussion voices where `NOTE_MAP` has 2,
independently corroborated by the manual's own HB/LB/LC rows). Fixing it
needs a new `NOTE_MAP` entry and, to be useful, a macro slot this
dossier's 16/16 budget doesn't have room for.

## 9. Change record — the SD fallback, taken

**Trigger:** the **cross-kit loudness grid**, not a listening call. Brad
declined to bless the cr78 *kit* at the 2026-09-02 re-bless listen without
naming a voice, and had approved this same snare on 2026-09-01; the grid is
what identified the snare, at -46.7 LUFS against a -21.1 kick in its own kit.
§5's SD row wrote its precondition as *"if this reads too thin by ear"*; that
precondition is **not** satisfied on the record, and the fallback is taken on
measurement instead. The distinction is kept because this is the one cr78
voice with no measured hardware reference of any kind, so the grade rests
entirely on how the decision is documented.

**What the fallback's funding clause said, and why it was not followed.** §5
nominated merging Claves+Cowbell to free the Note. That merge was built and
then rejected: it corrupts both voices' envelopes on overlap (table in §5).
cr78 carries a 14th Note instead.

**What changed in `cr78.py`** (nothing outside this module; `_support.py` and
every other instrument untouched):

1. **SD is 2 Notes again** — unfiltered SINE body at `SD Pitch` (decay 0.1 s,
   `amp * 0.55`) plus a HIGH_PASS 1800 Hz Q=1.0 noise path (decay 0.14 s,
   `amp * sd_snappy`). `SD Snappy` is the tone/noise blend again, not the
   filter's Q.
2. **Claves + Cowbell share one circuit**, waveform swapped in place per hit —
   the Note that funds SD, nominated by the SD row itself. The **Hat merge was
   left alone**: its two faces are cr78's only captured voices (§1) and already
   measure healthy.
3. **The arbiter counts circuits, not Notes** (§6), so the 2-Note snare still
   admits as one voice and the hardware's 4-sound ceiling holds.

**Measured, before → after** (shared BS.1770 ruler, 400 ms from onset,
velocity-100 solo one-shots; envelope/spectral figures from
`tools/measure_hits.py`):

| | before | after | peer field (8 kits) | nearest sibling: `drumtraks` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snare LUFS | **-46.7** | **-23.0** | -26.8 … -19.1 | -25.9 |
| Snare peak | -31.2 dBFS | -10.6 dBFS | -13.7 … -7.4 | -10.3 |
| tau | 0.0113 s | 0.0357 s | 0.022 … 0.084 s (median 0.036) | 0.036 |
| centroid | 2761 Hz | 11594 Hz | 4979 … 11780 Hz (median 7546) | 7529 Hz |
| `f_early` / `f_late` | 211 Hz / 20 Hz | 246 Hz / 3020 Hz | body, then rattle | — |
| energy < 500 Hz | 93.4% | 37.6% | 0.1 … 91.8% | 36.7% |
| energy > 2 kHz | 1.2% | 59.8% | 7.4 … 97.0% | 60.5% |

Every figure lands inside the peer field — re-measured this pass with
`tools/measure_hits.py` over the nine other kits' note-38 snares, because the
tau and centroid ranges first written here were wrong (tau read `0.037 … 0.051`,
which would have put our own 0.0357 outside the field it claimed to be inside).
The new snare's tau of 0.036 s is the peer **median** exactly. The band split is
within a point
of `drumtraks` — the closest structural sibling in the library, itself a tone
body plus a filtered-noise path. **One honest caveat:** at 11594 Hz the
centroid sits at the *bright* end of the field, just under `tr606`'s 11770. A
bare HIGH_PASS passes everything up to Nyquist, so the rattle is wideband; if
the batch listen finds it hissy rather than snappy, the move is to band-pass
the noise path instead of high-passing it (`tr909.py:200` does exactly that,
and lands at 8998 Hz), not to pull the level back down.

The `f_late` figure is the one that answers the actual complaint: the old voice
had **nothing** above the body 100 ms in — `f_late` reads 20 Hz, i.e. DC — and
the new one carries a 3 kHz rattle tail. This is a rebuilt circuit, not a gain
trim: the level and the spectrum moved together, which is the only thing that
distinguishes the two (§7).
