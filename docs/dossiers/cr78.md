## 3. Hardware structure vs the module

**The polyphony fact, sourced exactly:** Wikipedia states "The CR-78
offers 14 sounds, with four-note polyphony (meaning it can produce up to
four sounds at the same time)," attached to a footnote (re-confirmed this
session). The 14-sound list separately names **High conga, Low conga,
High bongo, Low bongo** as four distinct tuned-percussion voices (plus
Kick, Snare, Rimshot, Hi-hat, Cymbal and — per the module's own remaining
`NOTE_MAP` entries — Tambourine, Metal Beat, Cowbell, Guiro, Maracas,
Claves).

**Independently corroborated by the service notes, not just asserted from
Wikipedia:** the manual's own factory "RHYTHM VOICE" adjustment procedure
(OCR'd, `.reference-captures/cr78/service_notes_djvu.txt` lines ~6684-7170)
lists **BD, SD, RS, HH, CY, HB (High Bongo), LB (Low Bongo), LC, and CB
(Cowbell)** as separate rows, each with its own dedicated trim
potentiometer(s) (e.g. "VR51... VR52" for HB, "VR53... VR54" for LB,
"VR55... VR56" for **LC** — a *third*, distinct tuned-percussion row,
consistent with Wikipedia's separate Low Conga). A later line groups three
more voices for one adjustment step: *"Slide ADD VOICE knobs upward, {Tb,
GU, MB, respectively}"* (Tambourine, Guiro, Metal Beat). This is real,
independent, primary-source corroboration of two things: (a) each named
voice has its **own dedicated analog circuit** (not a shared-generator
architecture like `tr909`'s hi-hat/cymbal — no CR-78-specific evidence of
circuit sharing was found anywhere in the OCR text), and (b) **the
hardware likely has more tuned-percussion voices than the module's
`NOTE_MAP` currently carries.**

**A real, evidenced gap:** the module's `NOTE_MAP` (cr78.py:42-57) has
only two conga/bongo-family entries — `(60, "Bongo Hi")`, `(61, "Bongo
Lo")` (cr78.py:53-54) — where Wikipedia names four and the service notes'
own HB/LB/LC rows independently support at least three. This dossier does
**not** propose adding the missing voice(s): doing so needs a new
`NOTE_MAP` entry and, to stay useful, a new macro, and `MACRO_LABELS` is
already at 16/16 (§7) — noted here for whoever next revisits this
instrument, not solved in this minimal-change plan.

**What the manual does *not* explain, searched for explicitly and not
found:** the mechanism behind the 4-simultaneous ceiling. The OCR text was
searched for "simultaneous," "same time," "voice," "block diagram,"
"priority," "matrix," "latch," and "gate" (see the raw hits above). The
closest matches — **"ADD VOICE"** (a physical slider, part number
`LYE6B001-10KB`, labelled "VOL. ADD VOICE" in the switches list) and
**"CANCEL VOICE"** (a push-switch, `SUF53`, "CLEAR, CANCEL VOICE") — read
in context (lines 2260-2270, 7165-7166, 7649) as **rhythm-programming**
controls: selecting which instruments participate in a pattern via the
rotary `INSTRUMENT SELECTOR`, and clearing/cancelling one instrument's
programmed pattern during composition — not a real-time voice-steal
description. **Not found, stated honestly rather than guessed at:**
whether a 5th simultaneous trigger on real hardware is dropped, steals the
oldest voice, or does something else. §6 proposes a mechanism; it is not
sourced to this manual.

**Module today:** `MAX_VOICES = 16` (cr78.py:100) is a `synthio.Note`-pool
ceiling with no relationship to the hardware's real ceiling — 4x too high,
the mismatch the task flagged. Per Brad's "if it was 4-voice in real
life, it's 4-voice here" rule, §6 proposes a real-time 4-voice arbiter
replacing it.

## 4. The two budgets, and why CR-78 needs both

`tr909`/`tr707`/`sp1200` each needed only **one** number: how many
permanent circuits fit under the audioif-wide 13-note residency ceiling
(synthio's 14-note program-wide cap, minus one for `audioif#8`/`#9`'s
at-cap re-press bugs). Their hardware's own simultaneity ceiling and their
resident-circuit count were the same number (or close enough that no
separate arbiter was needed) — hit any pattern you like, everything can
sound at once, because that is what those machines' hardware did.

**CR-78 is the first Phase 1 drum where these two numbers genuinely
differ.** 14 named sounds (≈13-14 dedicated analog circuits, §3) need to
be *resident* so any of them can fire; but the reference caps *audible
simultaneity* at 4, well below that. Two separate mechanisms are needed:

1. **Residency (§5):** how many permanent `synthio.Note` objects exist,
   ≤13 (the audioif-wide ceiling, unchanged from the other three drums).
2. **The real-time arbiter (§6):** how many of those may be in an active
   (non-silent) envelope phase at once, capped at the reference's own 4 —
   a genuinely new mechanism this program hasn't needed before.

## 5. The FIXED-CIRCUIT map (mandatory)

Following the proven `tr909`/`sp1200` idiom exactly (`circuits = {}` cache
keyed by voice name, `circuit(name, waveforms)` builds each permanent
`synthio.Note` tuple once and returns the cached tuple on every later hit
— see `tr909.py:127-135` for the pattern this reuses verbatim): every
voice is a **permanent** `synthio.Note` group, parameters reset and
`synth.press()`-retriggered in place on every hit, matching how the real
machine's voltage-triggered analog circuits work — nothing allocated at
strike time, nothing freed mid-play.

| Voice | Resident Notes | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| BD | 1 | matches today; the manual's own BD row confirms a dedicated tunable circuit exists |
| SD | **1** (down from today's 2) | budget-driven, flagged: no CR-78-specific evidence of a dual-oscillator snare was found (unlike `tr909`'s confirmed IC37+IC38, §4 of that dossier) — a single resonant band-passed-noise Note carries both body and snap; `SD Snappy` (§7) repurposed as the filter's Q rather than a tone/noise blend. **Fallback if this reads too thin by ear:** restore the second (tone) oscillator and find the 1 note elsewhere (e.g. merge Claves+Cowbell, both simple single-hit resonators, in place of the Hat merge below) |
| RS | 1 | matches today; own row in the adjustment table |

## 6. The real-time 4-voice arbiter (the hardware's own limit, layered on top)

This dossier proposes **reusing the module's own existing
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest`/`release_voice` machinery
unchanged** (already imported at cr78.py:61-66, already wired at
cr78.py:105-116) — just feeding it the **cached, permanent** circuit Note
tuples from §5 instead of freshly-constructed ones (the functions are
agnostic to that distinction: `_support.py:199-214` and `:252-261` just
press/release whatever `notes` tuple they're handed), and changing what
`k` identifies (circuit name, not raw MIDI pitch, so Closed/Open Hat
collapse onto the Hat circuit's one arbiter slot if §5's merge holds).

**The number is 5, not 4 — verified by hand-tracing the existing loop,
not assumed:** `trigger_voice`'s stealing check is `while len(voices) +
len(notes) >= max_voices` (`_support.py:255`). With every CR-78 trigger
pressing exactly 1 note (true uniformly once SD and Guiro are both
reduced to 1 Note in §5 — a second reason for those two reductions,
beyond the residency budget), tracing five consecutive distinct triggers
against `MAX_VOICES = 4` lands at a **steady-state of 3** simultaneously
held voices (the check fires and steals down to 2 *before* the 4th is
even admitted). `MAX_VOICES = 5` is what actually realizes Wikipedia's
cited 4-voice ceiling: the 5th distinct trigger's check fires while only
3 are held, steals down to 2, then presses, landing at 4 held — matching
the reference. Retriggering an *already-held* circuit doesn't consume a
new slot (`trigger_voice` calls `release(k)` unconditionally first,
popping the same key before the count check), so hitting the same drum
twice never steals a different voice — the same behavior a
voltage-triggered analog circuit would show.

**Flagged plainly:** §3 found no description of the real mechanism.
Steal-oldest is proposed because it is the module's own already-tested
idiom, not because the manual specifies it. If the batch listen finds
stealing wrong (e.g. a real CR-78 might simply drop the 5th trigger
instead), swapping `trigger_voice`'s call for a no-op-on-full-arbiter is a
small, isolated change — the same "flagged, not asserted, degrades
gracefully" posture `tr909.md` used for its crash/ride choke.

## 8. MCU budget and macro budget

13 resident Notes (§5) — the same order as `tr909` (12) and `sp1200`
(12); **no `" - lean"` patch expected.** The Guiro redesign (5→1 Notes,
strong precedent) is the single biggest saving; the Hat merge and SD
reduction are the two lower-confidence calls that make up the rest, both
flagged in §5 with a stated fallback if either is refused at the batch
listen. The arbiter (§6) adds no new audioif nodes — it reuses
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest`/`release_voice`, already imported
(cr78.py:61-66), against a 5-entry-max dict instead of freely growing one.

`MACRO_LABELS` stays at **16/16** (cr78.py:9-13) — already at the
ceiling; **no new macro proposed.** Direct manual support exists for BD,
SD, RS, Bongo Hi, Bongo Lo, Cowbell, and Hat Tone (§3, §7); Accent follows
standard rhythm-composer convention and was not independently confirmed
in the sources read this session; the remaining "X Level" macros
(Claves, Guiro, Tambourine, Maracas, Metal Beat) are plausible but
likewise not independently confirmed. **One nuance worth recording
plainly:** the manual's switches list shows the real panel used a single
physical "VOL./ADD VOICE" slider together with the rotary `INSTRUMENT
SELECTOR` — select a voice, then trim its level on one shared control —
rather than 14 simultaneously-live knobs. Today's 16 independently-live
macros already exceed that literal 1970s ergonomics. That's explicitly
allowed under the macro-freedom rule (roadmap §3, vision §2.2) and is a
usability improvement, not a fidelity claim — recorded here only so it
isn't mistaken for a sourced 1:1 panel mapping.

**Not solved here, recorded for the next revisit:** §3's conga/bongo gap
(the reference names 4 tuned-percussion voices where `NOTE_MAP` has 2,
independently corroborated by the manual's own HB/LB/LC rows). Fixing it
needs a new `NOTE_MAP` entry and, to be useful, a macro slot this
dossier's 16/16 budget doesn't have room for.

**Operating mode v2:** built unattended; deviations stated in the
evidence pack; blessed at the phase batch listen.
