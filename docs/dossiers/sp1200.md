- **E-mu SP-1200 Service Manual Rev. 1 (1987)**, archive.org
  (https://archive.org/details/emu-sp-1200-service-manual-1987_202010).
  The item's OCR/text layers (`_djvu.txt`, `_hocr_searchtext.txt`, and the
  embedded PDF text layer) turn out to carry only a repeated copyright
  watermark — the actual content is scan images, not searchable text — so
  this dossier's manual citations come from directly reading rendered
  page images (`pages/page_44.png` etc., rendered locally via PyMuPDF
  since `pdftoppm`/poppler is not installed here; images deleted after
  reading, per the "downloads/measurements only" scope). Key quotes,
  cited by the manual's own printed page number:
  - p.39, Theory of Operation/Overview: "...major differences like the
    **12 bit format**, disk drive and user sampling."
  - p.40, Fig. 2 (Simplified Block Diagram): `Demux → S/H's → Filters →
    Mix Amp`, 8 parallel paths from the demultiplexer.
  - p.41: "The sound memory is composed entirely of 4464 Dynamic RAMs...
    for a total sound memory size of **256K words**. The sound memory is
    divided up into **4 banks of 2.5 seconds each**" (→ 262144 words /
    10 s ≈ **26,214 Hz**, this dossier's own derivation from the stated
    architecture — see §4). "The DAC is a **standard 12 bit linear
    device**." "For signal level control a standard **8 bit multiplying
    DAC** is used as an attenuator... to control the level of each sound
    separately." "The de-multiplexer separates the **8 channels**...
    **Two channels (7,8) are direct outs. The rest of the channels are
    filtered.**"
  - p.43: "The SP1200 uses a **Z-80A processor running at 3.3 mHz**."
  - Fig. 7 (Sample Circuits, p.44): signal path drawn as `Anti-Alias
    Filter → Sample/Hold → Comp. → SAR → ...`, confirming a real
    anti-aliasing stage precedes the 12-bit successive-approximation
    conversion.
- **Yeh, Nolting, Smith, "Physical and Behavioral Circuit Modeling of the
  SP-12 Sampler," ICMC 2007**
  (https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~dtyeh/papers/yeh07_icmc_sp12.pdf) — about
  the SP-1200's direct predecessor, which the SP-1200's own service
  manual (p.39, above) says it closely resembles. Read in full (4 pages).
  Load-bearing findings, re-derived below, not copied: 8-voice polyphony
  through 8 independent outputs; "ideal 12-bit A/D conversion... yielding
  a 72-dB noise floor"; the pitch mechanism is table-read-rate
  skipping/repeating with truncated (not interpolated) fractional index —
  "a sample may be repeated several times" when tuned down; **measured**
  length ratios on the real SP-12, exponential sine sweep excitation:
  tuned to highest pitch, the response is **2/3 the original 1.5 s
  length**; tuned to lowest pitch, **1.56× the original length** (Figs.
  6–7); the per-channel VCF's cutoff "follows an approximately
  exponential trajectory" after trigger with Q substantially unvaried
  (§3.6, SPICE + white-noise excitation); the spec'd 27.5 kHz sample rate
  was **measured** at 26 kHz on the real unit (§4) — independently
  landing in the same ~26 kHz band as this dossier's own 26,214 Hz manual
  derivation and Wikipedia/Lytrix's cited 26.04 kHz for the SP-1200 proper.
- **Lytrix/EMU-SP1200** reverse-engineered schematic/BOM
  (https://github.com/Lytrix/EMU-SP1200) — README read in full
  (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Lytrix/EMU-SP1200/main/README.md):
  corroborates 26,040 Hz / 12-bit and the skip/double-play pitch
  mechanism from independent reverse-engineering, not from the same
  manual.
- **Wikipedia, "E-mu SP-1200"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-1200)
  infobox: polyphony "**8 voices**," "**Fully multi-timbral**," filter
  "**SSM2044**, SSI2144 (2021 reissue)," synthesis "26.04 kHz 12-bit
  samples, drop-sample pitch-shifting"; body text: "SP-1200 provides
  additional unfiltered versions of the signals from its first six
  channels... using TRS connections... alternatively to their analog
  filters" — the SSM2044-on-six-of-eight-channels claim, read directly
  rather than taken on the earlier survey's word.

Literature clears its bar solidly: the service manual and the Yeh paper
together are sufficient to derive sample rate, bit depth, channel/filter
routing, the pitch-shift mechanism, and its length-scaling signature with
real numbers, not guesses.

## 3. Hardware structure vs the module

**Reference structure:** 8-voice polyphonic, fully multi-timbral, 8
independent hardware output channels (Wikipedia infobox; Yeh §1.1, "8-voice
polyphony through 8 independent outputs," describing the same
architecture family). The service manual's own block diagram (Fig. 2,
p.40) shows exactly 8 parallel `Demux → S/H → Filter → Mix` paths, and its
text (p.41) states plainly that channels 7 and 8 bypass the filter stage
while 1–6 do not — SSM2044 analog filters on **six** of the eight
channels, confirmed independently by Wikipedia's TRS-unfiltered-first-six
note. Eight is a hard architectural ceiling, not a convention: there is no
ninth channel to borrow.

**Module today:** `sp1200.py` has **no `MAX_VOICES` at all** — confirmed
by enumeration (`grep -n MAX_VOICES lib/audioinstruments/sp1200.py` — no
output) against every sibling drum module, all of which define one
(`grep -n MAX_VOICES lib/audioinstruments/*.py`: `cs80.py:81`,
`cp70.py:64`, `clavinet.py:69`, `dx7.py:84`, `jupiter8.py:79`,
`cr78.py:100`, `fairlight.py:82`, `karplus.py:142`, `arp2600.py:81`,
`juno106.py:84`). `handle_event` releases only the same key
(`sp1200.py:82`, `release_voice(k)`) and then presses new notes
unconditionally (`sp1200.py:115-119`), never calling
`_support.trigger_voice`/`steal_oldest` the way every capped sibling does
(`_support.py:252-260`). Distinct simultaneous keys are therefore
**uncapped** — the module can run past the hardware's 8-channel ceiling
in either direction (no voice limit at all, rather than the reference's
firm 8), and `NOTE_MAP` (`sp1200.py:32-37`) covers only 4 of the 8
channels the hardware actually has (Kick 36, Snare 38, Closed Hat 42,
Open Hat 46).

Proposed structural change, justified by §1's own citations, not
assumed: rebuild as a **fixed-circuit, 8-voice** instrument — one
permanent `synthio.Note` group per hardware channel, an explicit 8-voice
ceiling (matching the reference exactly, in the direction Brad's rule
requires: "if it was monophonic in real life, it's monophonic here"
generalizes to "if it had 8 channels in real life, it has 8 voice slots
here," not more and not fewer), with channels 1–6 carrying the modeled
SSM2044 filter and channels 7–8 bypassing it exactly as the manual states.

**A named gap:** the reference pack's own 65-file kit (which instrument
names actually fill those 8 channels on a factory SP-1200 disk) was never
reached — the acquisition gap in §1 is total. §4's proposed voice map is
built from the module's existing 4 roles plus the sibling note-number
convention (`tr808.md` §3 already documents `linndrum.py:55-57`,
`cr78.py:55`) and general SP-1200/hip-hop kit convention, **not** from the
pack's real file list. If the pack becomes reachable later (a manual
Klaviyo signup, like tr808's Splice account gap), Station B or C should
re-check role assignment against the real 65 names before treating this
as settled.

## 4. Modeling approach

**What synthio can and cannot reach, stated plainly (per the charter's
mandate — no bitcrusher node exists at the instrument layer):**

| Character | Reachable? | How |
|---|---|---|
| ~26.04 kHz effective bandwidth (Nyquist ≈13 kHz) | **Reachable** | a fixed low-pass stage (or a wide-biquad band edge) capping synthesized content near 13 kHz on every voice, representing the anti-alias filter's own ceiling (Fig. 7, p.44) rather than a tone knob |
| 12-bit quantization noise floor (~72 dB SNR, Yeh) | **Reachable as a proxy only** | a fixed, low-level broadband noise mixed under each voice, tuned so the tail sits near -72 dBFS relative to full scale — *not* true bit-depth-dependent quantization noise, which synthio has no primitive for |
| 8-bit level-DAC quantization (p.41) | **Reachable, exactly** | quantize the pressed amplitude (`amp` in today's code, `sp1200.py:84`) to 256 discrete steps before `synth.press` — this one is not an approximation, the real DAC is 8-bit |
| Drop-sample pitch → decay-length coupling (Yeh §3.5, Figs. 6-7) | **Reachable, as a coupling, not as true resampling** | today's `hz` and `env.decay_time`/`bend` are set independently per voice (`sp1200.py:91-97` etc.) — bind `decay_time` (and any `bend`/LFO rate) to scale as `1/pitch_ratio` against a reference pitch, so raising `Kick Pitch` shortens the voice the way raising playback rate shortens a fixed-length wavetable read. Yeh's measured SP-12 ratios (2/3× at max pitch-up, 1.56× at max pitch-down) are the working curve in the absence of the SP-1200's own pack |
| SSM2044 VCF: exponential cutoff sweep post-trigger, near-constant Q (Yeh §3.6) | **Reachable** | the codebase already has the idiom: `_support.release_filter` (`_support.py:217-234`) builds exactly this shape (one-shot `LFO` → `Math(SCALE_OFFSET)` → `Biquad` frequency) for release tails; the same construction at *attack* time, applied to channels 1–6 only, models the SSM2044 sweep |
| True aliasing artifacts (spurious tones from the truncated-index skip/repeat and the zero-order-hold's imaging, both documented in Yeh §3.3/§3.4) | **Not reachable** | these require actual non-interpolated fractional-index table reads and a real sample-and-hold reconstruction step; synthio's oscillators/wavetables do neither, and there is no bitcrusher/sample-rate-reduction node at the instrument layer. Stated as unreachable, not faked |

**Per-voice notes**, all quantities keyed to §3's proposed 8-channel map
(channels 1–6 filtered, 7–8 direct, per the manual):

- **Kick** (`sp1200.py:90-97`, ch.1, filtered) — keep the SINE + optional
  PULSE-under-crunch pair (2 resident Notes), add the pitch→decay coupling
  above, add the attack-time VCF sweep in place of the current static
  `lp` cutoff (`sp1200.py:94`).
- **Snare** (`sp1200.py:99-106`, ch.2, filtered) — keep the body+snap pair
  (2 resident Notes); the two-Note split approximates one recorded
  sample's spectrum, since on the real hardware a snare hit is a single
  recorded sample, not a live-triggered stack — worth stating plainly so
  a future reader doesn't mistake the split for a hardware fact.
- **Closed/Open Hat** (`sp1200.py:108-113`, ch.3/4, filtered) — 1 Note
  each, unchanged in count; gains the VCF sweep and bandlimit.
- **Rimshot** (new, ch.5, filtered, note 37 — sibling convention per
  `tr808.md` §3/`linndrum.py:55-57`/`cr78.py:55`-style numbering) — 1
  Note, noise-through-bandpass, matching the module's existing hat/snap
  idiom.
- **Clap** (new, ch.6, filtered, note 39) — 1 Note. Unlike the TR-808's
  three-stage analog clap circuit (`tr808.md` §4, unaffected by this
  dossier), the SP-1200's clap is one recorded sample on one channel — a
  single resident Note is *more* accurate here, not a corner cut.
- **Tom** (new, ch.7, direct/unfiltered, note 41) — 1 Note. Single
  generic tom pending the real kit list; toms are usually filtered
  sounds by ear, but the manual is explicit that channels 7-8 bypass the
  filter regardless of what is loaded there, so this dossier follows the
  hardware's channel assignment over the more "natural"-sounding one.
- **Cowbell** (new, ch.8, direct/unfiltered, note 56) — 1 Note.

Total resident notes: Kick 2 + Snare 2 + CH 1 + OH 1 + Rim 1 + Clap 1 +
Tom 1 + Cowbell 1 = **10**, plus the noise-floor proxy layered onto Kick
and Snare only (+2) = **12 resident `synthio.Note` objects**, one below
the task's 13-note ceiling (synthio's cross-runtime 14-note cap, minus one
for headroom on retrigger — the same margin `tr808-evidence.md` used).

**Toolkit note:** no convolution is proposed (no `audioconvolve.FRAMES`
latency incurred). The VCF sweep and the bandlimit both reuse
`synthio.Biquad`/`LFO`/`Math`, already proven cheap in this codebase via
`_support.release_filter`; `audiodynamics` is not needed for this voice
set (no compression/limiting character is documented for the SP-1200's
signal path in anything read this session).

**"SP Crunch" — kept, rebound to something measurable.** As written
today it is a grab-bag (adds volume, opens the kick's low-pass, adds a
pulse layer, and *lowers* the hat's high-pass — `sp1200.py:84,94,96-97,
103,111`) with no single SP-1200 control behind it. The real hardware has
no "crunch" knob. This dossier rebinds the label to the one real,
measurable thing it can honestly stand for: **the depth of the SSM2044
attack-time cutoff sweep** described in Yeh §3.6 — 0 gives a static
cutoff, higher values widen the sweep's starting point above the resting
cutoff. This is real (it is the VCF's own documented behavior), it is
SP-1200-specific (not a generic "make it grittier" knob), and it is
measurable the same way Yeh measured it: excite with noise, trace cutoff
vs. time.

## 5. Acceptance criteria (literature-derived — no measured pack this session)

| Criterion | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Effective bandwidth ceiling | no synthesized energy meaningfully above ≈13 kHz (Nyquist of ≈26 kHz) | manual p.41 memory-math derivation (256K words / 10 s ≈ 26,214 Hz), cross-checked against Wikipedia/Lytrix's 26.04 kHz and Yeh §4's measured ~26 kHz on the sibling SP-12 |

| Level quantization | pressed amplitude falls on one of 256 discrete steps | manual p.41, "8 bit multiplying DAC... to control the level of each sound separately" |
| Pitch-tied decay length | at max pitch-up, decay ≈0.6–0.7× nominal; at max pitch-down, decay ≈1.5–1.6× nominal | Yeh Figs. 6–7 (SP-12 measured: 2/3× and 1.56×) — sibling-architecture anchor, pending the SP-1200 pack itself |

| Channel/filter routing | 6 of 8 voices (Kick, Snare, CH, OH, Rim, Clap) carry the VCF; 2 (Tom, Cowbell) bypass it | manual p.41, "Two channels (7,8) are direct outs... the rest... are filtered" |

## 6. MCU budget and macro budget

Today's module is cheap: at most 2 ad-hoc `synthio.Note` objects per hit,
1-2 `Biquad`s, no resident state. The rebuild moves to **12 permanently
resident Notes** (§4) plus 6 attack-time VCF sweep generators (one
`LFO`+`Math`+`Biquad` triple per filtered channel, reusing the
`release_filter` idiom already paid for elsewhere in this codebase) plus
2 static low-pass stages on the direct channels for the bandwidth
ceiling. This is the same shape of increase tr808's fixed-circuit rebuild
already carried (ad-hoc → resident) without needing a `" - lean"` patch,
so **none is expected here either**. If Station C's MCU measurement says
otherwise, the stated fallback is dropping the VCF sweep on the four new
percussion voices (Rim/Clap/Tom/Cowbell keep a static filter, only
Kick/Snare/CH/OH keep the sweep) rather than cutting voices or macros.

`MACRO_LABELS` stays at **8 of 16** — no new macro is proposed. All eight
trace to something real or to a stated measured need:

| # | Macro | Traces to |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Volume | the mix-out pot (manual p.39: "The mix out pot is connected in the feedback loop of the mix out buffer amp") |
| 1 | Kick Pitch | per-channel Tune |
| 2 | Kick Ring | measured need — extra tail beyond the Yeh-anchored pitch-coupled decay, pending the real pack's own tau |
| 3 | Snare Pitch | per-channel Tune |
| 4 | Snare Snap | measured need — balance of the two-Note synthesis approximation of one recorded snare sample (§4) |
| 5 | Hihat Pitch | per-channel Tune (shared CH/OH, as today) |
| 6 | SP Crunch | rebound to the SSM2044 attack-sweep depth (§4) — real and measurable, not the prior grab-bag |
| 7 | Master Tune | global fine-tune, standard sampler function |

The four new percussion voices (Rim, Clap, Tom, Cowbell) get fixed tuning
with no dedicated macro, to stay inside this budget without adding macros
that don't yet trace to anything — 8 slots of headroom remain to 16 if
Station C's real measurements later demand independent tuning for them.
